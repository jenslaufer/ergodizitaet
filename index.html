Decision game · two days · about 8 minutes

You have 1,000 kroner. What will you do with them?

Two days, both with the same instructions. First you watch nine images move your wealth. Then you choose, again and again, between two gambles — without ever being told how they turned out. At the end of each day, ten of your gambles are drawn at random and settled.

This is the procedure of a real experiment from the research literature. Which one is revealed at the end — read beforehand, it would colour your decisions.

How it works

  1. You start each day with 1,000 DKK.
  2. Passive phase. Nine image stimuli, each with a fixed effect on your wealth. Every image is shown 37 times, 333 trials in total. Nothing to decide — just watch and learn what each image does.
  3. Active phase. 312 choices between two gambles. Each gamble is made of two of the images you learned, each with 50 % probability. The outcomes stay hidden.
  4. At the end of the day, 10 of your chosen gambles are drawn at random and applied to your wealth. That is the payout.
  5. The second day is the same: same instructions, new images.

The original had 312 decisions per day. The less you play, the fuzzier your own estimate at the end — the page will tell you how fuzzy.

The reveal

The two days were not the same. On one day your wealth was added to: each image stood for a fixed amount between −428 and +428 kr. On the other it was multiplied: each image stood for a factor between 0.447 and 2.239.

You were not told, and that was not an oversight. The participants in the original were not told either: same instructions, same procedure, same scanner, and the order of the two days was balanced across the group. What is being measured is whether your risk attitude shifts with the dynamic — without you knowing there was one.

What you played is the Copenhagen experiment of 2017 at the DRCMR: Meder, Rabe, Morville, Madsen, Koudahl, Dolan, Siebner, Hulme and Peters, published in 2021. 18 people, two days in an MRI scanner, real money. The full citation is at the bottom of this page.

The nine stimuli

Additive: amounts from −428 to +428 DKK, evenly spaced on a linear scale. Multiplicative: growth factors from 0.447 (roughly halving) to 2.239 (roughly doubling), evenly spaced on a logarithmic scale. Both series were recomputed from the paper's raw data, not guessed from the prose.

Your risk aversion

Your choices are fitted with the same model the paper uses: isoelastic utility with a single parameter eta. eta = 0 means risk neutral (linear utility), eta = 1 means logarithmic utility. What ergodicity theory predicts: eta = 0 on the additive day, eta = 1 on the multiplicative one — because that is what maximises the growth rate over time, rather than the expected value.

Where this rebuild is not 1:1

  • {{ nAktiv }} decisions instead of 312 per day, and {{ passivSeq.length }} passive trials instead of 333. That is why your eta stands on thin ground: ±{{ komma(etaFehler, 2) }} (curvature of the likelihood).
  • No real money. The original paid out up to 4,000 DKK, on top of a 1,020 DKK participation fee.
  • No MRI scanner, and not two separate days. Participants lay in the scanner for three hours on each of two different dates.
  • Different images. The original used 18 fixed fractal images, here they are generated patterns.
  • Maximum likelihood instead of hierarchical Bayesian estimation. The paper estimates eta and beta jointly across all participants; here your eta is estimated on its own, without a prior.
  • The gamble pairs come from the same restricted space as in the original (both gambles mixed, all four images different, plus {{ nNoBrainer }} no-brainer trials as a control) — just not all 144 of them.
